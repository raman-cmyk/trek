Langtang is the closest big-mountain valley to Kathmandu — a day's drive, no flight — and the least crowded of Nepal's classic treks. Eight days takes you from subtropical forest to glacier country at almost 5,000m, through villages that rebuilt themselves after the 2015 earthquake and greet trekkers like the lifeline they are.

## How long it takes

The standard itinerary is 7–8 days: drive to Syabrubesi, walk up the valley over three days to Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m), take a day for Kyanjin Ri or Tserko Ri (4,984m), and return the way you came. No flights, no flight-weather roulette — which makes Langtang the most reliable trek in Nepal to actually finish on schedule.

## How hard it is

Moderate. The days are shorter than Everest Base Camp and the maximum sleeping altitude is lower, but the trail climbs steadily and the optional summits are genuinely steep. If you can walk 4–5 hours a day, Langtang is within reach — it is many people's first Himalayan trek for good reason.

## The best time to go

Spring (March–May) and autumn (October–November). Spring adds rhododendron forest in full bloom lower down. Winter is possible for the hardy — cold but often clear.

## The earthquake, and why your visit matters

The 2015 earthquake buried Langtang village and took over 300 lives. The valley rebuilt itself teahouse by teahouse, and trekking income is the engine of that recovery. Booking a local guide here isn't charity — it's the best trek nearest to Kathmandu — but your money lands where it matters more than almost anywhere else in Nepal.

Q: Is Langtang Valley good for a first Himalayan trek?
A: Yes — arguably the best. Moderate difficulty, no internal flights, real altitude without extreme altitude, and quiet trails.

Q: How do I get to the Langtang trailhead?
A: A 7–8 hour drive from Kathmandu to Syabrubesi. Your guide arranges the shared jeep or private transfer; there is no flying involved.

Q: How high does the Langtang Valley trek go?
A: You sleep at 3,870m (Kyanjin Gompa). The optional sunrise climb of Tserko Ri reaches 4,984m — the highest point of the trek.
