Everest Base Camp is the walk to the foot of the highest mountain on earth. It is not a climb — you never touch a rope — but it is fourteen days of walking between 2,800m and 5,644m, and the altitude is the whole challenge. Done at the right pace, with a guide who has walked it a hundred times, it is one of the great trails of the world.

## How long it takes

The classic itinerary is 12–14 days on the trail: fly into Lukla, walk up the Khumbu valley with two acclimatisation days (Namche and Dingboche), touch Base Camp and climb Kala Patthar for the sunrise view, then descend in three days. Rushing it is the single most common mistake — and the reason people get sick.

## How hard it is

If you can walk 5–6 hours a day on hills, you can do Everest Base Camp. The difficulty is altitude, not technical terrain. Above 4,000m every step is slower. A good guide watches your oxygen levels, sets the pace, and knows when to descend — which is exactly why we sell you the guide, not a package.

## The best time to go

Two seasons: **spring (March–May)** and **autumn (October–November)**. Autumn has the clearest skies and the stable weather; spring is warmer and greener lower down. Monsoon (June–September) and deep winter are possible but harder.

## Altitude and safety

Acclimatisation is non-negotiable. Our guides carry an oximeter, build in rest days, and will turn you around if your body says so. Every trek has daily check-ins, and travel insurance that covers helicopter evacuation above 4,000m is required — not optional.

Q: Do I need previous trekking experience for Everest Base Camp?
A: No. You need reasonable fitness and the willingness to go slowly. Most first-time Himalayan trekkers do fine with the right guide and pace.

Q: Is Everest Base Camp dangerous?
A: The main risk is altitude sickness, which is managed by acclimatising properly. With a licensed guide, daily check-ins, and evacuation insurance, it is a well-supported trek.
