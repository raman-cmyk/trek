Gokyo is the Khumbu without the crowds: the same flight to Lukla, the same Sherpa villages low down — then, where the Everest highway turns right, you turn left, up a quieter valley to a chain of turquoise glacial lakes at 4,700–5,000m. From the summit of Gokyo Ri you see four 8,000m peaks in one sweep, including a view of Everest many trekkers rate above the one from Kala Patthar.

## How long it takes

Twelve days is standard: fly to Lukla, acclimatise in Namche like the Base Camp crowds, then branch north-west through Dole and Machhermo to Gokyo (4,790m). A pre-dawn climb of Gokyo Ri (5,357m) is the summit day. Return down-valley — or, with more time and a strong forecast, cross the Cho La pass to join the Everest Base Camp trail.

## How hard it is

Comparable to Everest Base Camp: the altitude is nearly identical and the challenge is the same — thin air, not technical ground. The trail is quieter, which cuts both ways: fewer teahouses to choose from, and a guide who knows which ones are open matters more, not less.

## The best time to go

Spring (March–May) and autumn (October–November). The lakes are at their unreal bluest in late autumn under stable skies. In winter the lakes freeze — beautiful, but brutally cold at 4,800m.

## The lakes and the glacier

The six Gokyo lakes are sacred in both Hindu and Buddhist tradition — the third lake, beside the village, has a shrine where pilgrims gather each August. Beside them flows the Ngozumpa glacier, the longest in the Nepal Himalaya. Your guide will walk you onto its moraine safely; wandering onto glacier ice alone is how accidents happen.

Q: Is Gokyo Lakes better than Everest Base Camp?
A: It's quieter, the lakes are stunning, and the Gokyo Ri panorama is arguably wider than Kala Patthar's. You give up standing at Base Camp itself. Many of our guides quietly prefer Gokyo.

Q: Can I combine Gokyo Lakes with Everest Base Camp?
A: Yes — via the Cho La pass (5,420m), adding 3–4 days. It's a genuine high pass that needs good weather and a guide; discuss it when you message yours.

Q: How high is Gokyo Ri?
A: 5,357m. The climb from Gokyo village takes 2–3 hours before dawn — cold, slow, and worth every step at sunrise.
