The Annapurna Circuit loops around the entire Annapurna massif, climbing from subtropical rice terraces to the high, dry, Tibetan-feeling world above 4,000m, and over the Thorong La pass at 5,416m. It is the most varied long trek in Nepal — the landscape changes completely every two days.

## How long it takes

A full circuit is 14–18 days depending on where you start and stop; road-building has shortened the classic route, and a good guide knows which sections to walk and which to jeep past so you keep the best of it. Add the Tilicho Lake side trip if you have the days and the legs.

## How hard it is

Longer and higher than most first treks, with one big test: the Thorong La pass. It is a long, cold, pre-dawn climb to 5,416m and down the other side. The rest is steady walking with real acclimatisation gains built in. Fitness plus patience gets you across.

## The best time to go

**October–November** is prime: stable weather, clear passes, golden light. **March–May** is the spring alternative, warmer with rhododendrons in bloom. The Thorong La can close in heavy snow, so shoulder-season timing matters — another reason a local guide earns their fee.

## Altitude and safety

The Thorong La is the crux for altitude. Guides pace the approach, watch for symptoms, and have the judgement to hold a day or turn back if the weather or your body demands it. Daily check-ins and evacuation-capable insurance apply here too.

Q: How fit do I need to be for the Annapurna Circuit?
A: Fitter than for a short trek — you are walking most days for two weeks and crossing a 5,416m pass. If you train with hill walking beforehand, you will enjoy it far more.

Q: What permits do I need for the Annapurna Circuit?
A: The Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) and a TIMS card. Both are arranged for you; the current costs are shown in the table on this page.

Q: Is the Thorong La pass safe?
A: In good weather and with proper acclimatisation, yes. The risks are altitude and snow on the pass, both of which a licensed guide manages by timing the crossing and pacing the approach.
