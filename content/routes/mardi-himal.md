Mardi Himal is the best five days you can spend in the Himalaya. A single forested ridge climbs straight out of the Pokhara valley to 4,500m, dead-ending under the unclimbable fishtail summit of Machhapuchhre — with the whole Annapurna wall filling the horizon. It's short, it's steep, and it fits inside a one-week holiday including the flight to Pokhara.

## How long it takes

Five days on the trail: drive from Pokhara to the trailhead, two days climbing through rhododendron and cloud forest to Low Camp and High Camp (3,580m), a pre-dawn push to the Upper Viewpoint (about 4,500m), then down in a day. Add a rest day in Pokhara on either side and it's still barely a week.

## How hard it is

Moderate — but honest about it: the days are short in hours and relentless in gradient. You gain over 3,000m from the trailhead to the viewpoint. There are no easy flat days; there are also no dangerous ones. For fit walkers short on time, nothing in Nepal delivers more mountain per day.

## The best time to go

The most forgiving window of any trek here: February–May and October–December all work. Spring turns the entire ridge into a rhododendron corridor. Because the trek is short and low commitment, it's also the best monsoon-shoulder gamble — a two-day weather window is enough.

## Teahouses on the ridge

The lodges are newer and simpler than the Annapurna Circuit's — High Camp is a clutch of stone buildings in the cloud. Beds fill up in October; a guide who knows the owners is the difference between a room and a dining-bench night.

Q: Is Mardi Himal good for beginners?
A: Yes, for fit beginners. The altitude tops out around 4,500m on a day-trip basis (you sleep lower), which keeps the risk manageable while still being a real Himalayan summit morning.

Q: How many days do I need for Mardi Himal including travel?
A: Seven, door to door from Kathmandu: fly or drive to Pokhara, five days on the trail, return. It's the classic "I only have a week" answer.

Q: Can I see Everest from Mardi Himal?
A: No — this is the Annapurna range. You see Machhapuchhre close enough to touch, plus Annapurna South and Hiunchuli. It's a different postcard, not a lesser one.
